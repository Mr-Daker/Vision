What it does

What it does

Four things, end to end

#

Report in your own words

A photo, a voice note or a sentence. You are never asked to pick a department, a category or a severity — that is decided after review, not by you.

*

Duplicates are grouped

Reports of the same problem are joined into one issue, so ten neighbours reporting one blocked drain is one problem with ten voices, not ten tickets.

<

Routed to an owner

Each issue is matched to the department responsible for that category, and the decision is recorded so it can be re-derived later. Every department here is simulated.

%

Followed to an answer

You can see what happened to your report. A repair claim is not a verified resolution: it has to be confirmed by people who reported the problem.

About the project

Built to be checkable

Vision is a citizen reporting platform for public infrastructure, built for the Code for Communities challenge. The hard part of this problem is not collecting reports — it is being honest about what the system does and does not know.

Nothing claims to be official

Identity is simulated. Departments are simulated. No government system is contacted, and a receipt says plainly that it is not an official acknowledgement.

Absence is not evidence

A photo with its metadata stripped is the ordinary case, not a suspicious one. A consistency check that cannot conclude says so, and never counts against the person who reported.

No invented numbers

There is no confidence score and no severity rating, because nothing here has been calibrated to produce one. An AI category is a proposal for a person to confirm.

Two languages, one bar

English and Marathi. The Marathi pack is machine-drafted and the interface says so on screen, because an unreviewed translation presented as finished is its own kind of false claim.
